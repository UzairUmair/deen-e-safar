## Review performed

All original 53 figural assets were opened individually at original 1448 × 1086 resolution. Faces, profiles, background figures, reflections, clothing and context were inspected. No recognizable eyes, nose or mouth remained in the retained 28. They are anonymous artistic scenes, never portraits of a named Companion. Runtime labels explicitly state that historical illustrations are neither authentic portraits nor historical evidence, with translated disclosures.

The other 297 originals were inspected on 19 labeled review sheets. No further faces, misleading generated religious lettering or category mismatch was identified. The 25 prohibited Prophet-related figures were removed through targeted image edits preserving their lesson IDs and environments. Every edited original was inspected individually before approval. No people, silhouettes, back-facing bodies or human stand-ins remain in the Prophets category. This review reports observed visual content, not archaeological verification.

The manifest retains per-image prompts, outputs, hashes, descriptions, alt text, lesson/step associations, face status and final-QA method. The 25 replacement histories preserve prior metadata. Historical-review status remains `thematic-only-not-historical-evidence`; no asset was promoted to scholar-verified. Historical lessons with insufficient source coverage remain gated.

## Associations and presentation

Stable IDs make selection deterministic. Covers now explicitly map Badr to the well basin (`events-002`), Uhud to a mountain environment (`events-011`), Khandaq to earthworks (`events-021`) and Yusuf to a nonfigurative night-sky environment (`prophets-002`). Step associations remain intact. Quran/Hadith/Fundamentals use their own educational environments. These are contextual illustrations, not reconstructions of documented places or events.

The Academy hero, seven category covers, lesson previews, step artwork and optional galleries were exercised. The gallery initially renders four related illustrations and reveals more on request. It does not download the full collection on entry. Broken artwork shows a neutral unavailable state rather than an unrelated historical image.

## Files, duplicates and optimization

There are 713 public WebP files: 350 originals, 350 derivatives and 13 retained legacy fallbacks/hero images. Approved original bytes total 89,057,694. All current originals decode at 1200 × 900; derivatives decode at 600 × 450. They were downsampled from larger generated originals, not upscaled. SHA-256 hashes are unique. dHash screening found no pair at distance ≤5; visually distinct scenes may still share a category's style and motifs.

Cards use responsive `srcset`/`sizes`; explicit dimensions reserve space. Heroes receive appropriate priority, other images load lazily, and the static export uses local WebP without requiring a server image optimizer. Desktop Learn image transfer measured 668,922 bytes versus 1,855,684 before responsive-card selection (about 64% less). See the readiness report for benchmark scope.

Twenty-five superseded original/derivative pairs and three rejected public outputs were moved to ignored `.openai/final-qa/retired/`. They are no longer served. Manifest archive references explain each removal. The 13 legacy files are retained because existing fallback/hero paths use them; approved gallery assets were not deleted merely because they are below the fold.

No remaining violation of the final visual policy was observed. Remaining limitation: these thematic illustrations cannot establish historical authenticity.
